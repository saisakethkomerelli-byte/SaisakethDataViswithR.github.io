Objective of Exercise: The objective of this exercise was to choose a data visualisation and analyse them based on preattentive processing, gestalt laws and visual comparison accuracy.

1. What aspect of your chosen visualisation first grabbed your attention? Explain the feature in terms of preattentive processing. 

What grabs my attention most in this data visualisation is the cluster of red lines in the bottom right corner of the wagon wheel. They are closer to each other then the what the other coloured lines are and the brightness of the colour red in contrast to the white background makes them stand out. The use of colour in this visualisation is an attribute of preattentive processing as humans can recognise differences in colour without a conscious effort. The other colours blue, yellow and grey are also noticeable and we can differentiate what each line means with the help of the key.  

2. Explain one Gestalt law that your chosen visualisation demonstrates. 

 

A gestalt law demonstrated in the data visualisation above is proximity. As noticed before there is a cluster of red lines in the bottom right corner of the data visualisation. Observers of the graphic would naturally think of them as a related group. The cluster of red lines here suggests that Glenn Maxwell is more comfortable with hitting his aggressive shots in that area of the ground. Proximity helps us to group the lines which helps us identify patterns in his shot selection rather than attempting to analyse each individual line. 

 

3. Choose one of the quantitative variables visualised and discuss its visual accuracy. 

 

The length of each line is a quantitative variable that is visualised. On thie graphic we can see that each line has a different length depending on how many runs were scored off of that ball. The direction of these lines is accurate but the length is not as some 6s and 4s go much further over the boundary and some only just touch it. The same goes 1s, 2s and 3s, they all get hit to different lengths of the ground. But the length of the lines in the graphic is present to categorise the shots that were scored off. So, the length of the lines is inaccurate in terms of how far the ball landed but is accurate in the aim to categorise all of the scoring shots.
